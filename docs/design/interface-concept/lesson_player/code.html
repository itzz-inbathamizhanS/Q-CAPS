LESSON 3.2

How Shor's algorithm threatens RSA and ECC

Learning Objective

Explain quantum threats to classical cryptography and specifically how Shor's algorithm undermines the mathematical foundations of current public-key systems.

Classical cryptography, specifically public-key algorithms like RSA and Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC), rely on mathematical problems that are currently computationally infeasible for classical computers to solve in a reasonable timeframe. For RSA, this is the integer factorization problem; for ECC, it's the discrete logarithm problem.

FIG 1: QUANTUM THREAT LANDSCAPE

In 1994, Peter Shor formulated a quantum algorithm that, running on a sufficiently powerful and error-corrected quantum computer, can solve both integer factorization and discrete logarithm problems in polynomial time. This theoretically renders RSA and ECC completely broken.

warning The Mathematical Threat

Shor's algorithm uses quantum superposition and entanglement to find the period of a function, which exponentially speeds up the process of finding prime factors compared to the best known classical algorithms (like the General Number Field Sieve).

Classical Time Complexity: O(e^(c * (log N)^(1/3) * (log log N)^(2/3)))
Quantum Time Complexity (Shor's): O((log N)^3)

dictionary Key Terms

Shor's algorithm

A quantum computer algorithm for finding the prime factors of an integer, exponentially faster than classical algorithms.

RSA

Rivest-Shamir-Adleman. A widely used public-key cryptosystem relying on the difficulty of factoring large integers.

ECC

Elliptic Curve Cryptography. Public-key cryptography based on the algebraic structure of elliptic curves over finite fields.
