How Shor's Algorithm Threatens RSA and ECC

Understanding the mathematical mechanism that allows quantum computers to break traditional asymmetric encryption in polynomial time.

warning

The Fundamental Threat

RSA relies on the difficulty of factoring large integers, while ECC relies on the discrete logarithm problem. Classical computers take exponential time to solve these. Shor's algorithm, however, reduces the time complexity to polynomial time by leveraging quantum superposition and interference to find the period of a modular function.

RSA Vulnerability Integer Factorization

Shor's algorithm attacks RSA by finding the prime factors of the public modulus $N$.

// Simplified period finding phase
def shor_rsa_attack(N):
  a = random(2, N-1)
  # Quantum routine to find period 'r'
  r = quantum_period_finding(a, N)
  if r % 2 == 0:
    p = gcd(a**(r/2) - 1, N)
    return p, N/p

ECC Vulnerability Discrete Logarithm

ECC is theoretically broken faster than RSA because the key sizes are smaller, requiring fewer logical qubits.

menu_book Glossary & Notes

Period Finding

A mathematical problem that involves finding the smallest positive integer $r$ such that $f(x) = f(x+r)$ for all $x$. Shor's algorithm solves this efficiently on a quantum computer.

QFT

Quantum Fourier Transform. A linear transformation on quantum bits, analogous to the discrete Fourier transform. It is the key ingredient that allows Shor's algorithm to extract the period from the quantum state.

CRQC

Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer. A theoretical quantum computer powerful enough (with enough logical, error-corrected qubits) to break modern public-key cryptography.
